# Evidence and Inference Architecture

## Purpose

Make analytical PPT expression deeper while keeping every material claim and inferred relationship bounded by the source.

## 1. Evidence serves a claim or inference

Evidence may serve these roles:

- `claim` — directly supported factual statement;
- `reason` — explains why a judgment is valid;
- `mechanism` — explains how one state leads to another;
- `number` — scale, target, comparison, change or constraint;
- `instance` — example, case or manifestation;
- `authority` — policy, standard or institutional basis;
- `boundary` — condition, exclusion, responsibility, status or prerequisite;
- `trace_only` — provenance for audit.

## 2. Preserve claim strength

Never upgrade:

- suggestion to requirement;
- plan to completed result;
- possibility to commitment;
- pilot to full-scale implementation;
- correlation to causality without a supported mechanism;
- one member's evidence to a group-wide conclusion;
- sufficient support to a necessary-condition claim;
- internal estimate to public commitment or official statistic.

## 3. Explicit / inferred / speculative

### Explicit

The source directly states the relationship. It may be used as written, subject to normal compression.

### Inferred

Several source facts jointly support the relationship without requiring a new external premise. Inferred relations are allowed when they record:

- `support` fact IDs;
- `confidence`;
- a relationship statement no stronger than the evidence.

### Speculative

The relation requires an unstated fact, new assumption, unsupported ranking, forecast or universalization. Speculative relations do not enter authoritative Foundation relations, page argument chains or visual arrows.

## 4. Inference boundary test

For every newly created analytical judgment, ask:

1. Which source facts support it?
2. What unstated premise is required?
3. Does the inference change a fact's certainty, scope or status?
4. Does it create a new necessary condition or causal mechanism?
5. Would a reasonable reader accept the relationship from the cited facts alone?

If an external premise is essential, remove or weaken the inference.

## 5. Group-claim protection

When several peer items are summarized together, test each member before using a group-wide claim.

Example failure pattern:

- five service directions have documented long-term accumulation;
- one direction is only identified as an initial priority;
- the page says all six have long-term accumulation.

The group claim must use the lowest common supported strength or state the exception.

## 6. Visibility boundary

Foundation items may carry:

- `external_ok`;
- `internal_only`;
- `restricted`;
- `unspecified`.

For `audience_scope: external`, `internal_only` evidence must not appear in page message, full copy, onscreen copy or speaker notes unless the user explicitly authorizes exposure. It may remain in internal proof records.

For mixed audiences, PLAN must make an explicit visibility decision when using internal-only material.

## 7. Proof chains

A strong page normally follows:

`message -> supporting claim(s) -> evidence / mechanism / boundary -> bounded implication`

A taxonomy is also a valid page structure when classification itself is the source-supported relationship. Do not fabricate a process merely because arrows feel stronger visually.

## 8. Anti-loss rule

No source-critical item may disappear silently. It must be:

1. assigned to a page;
2. intentionally reserved for a later page or appendix; or
3. explicitly excluded for a user-approved reason.

Critical items include material numbers, responsibilities, status, policy strength, exclusions, prerequisites, rights, scope, commitments and facts needed by the source's own chapter logic.

## 9. Audience-facing evidence selection

Source references and evidence IDs are traceability. AUTHOR selects only audience-useful facts, numbers, cases and boundaries for visible copy. Internal proof material can remain hidden from the final-facing script when exposure is inappropriate.

## 10. Critic triggers

Rewrite when:

- a relation has no identifiable fact support;
- a group-wide claim exceeds one member's support;
- a page turns classification into sequence without evidence;
- a page introduces an unsupported current state or numeric gap;
- a plausible implication is written as fact;
- internal-only material leaks into external-facing prose;
- the strongest source boundary disappears during compression;
- the page has rich facts but no defensible analytical structure when one is available.
