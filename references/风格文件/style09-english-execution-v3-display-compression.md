# STYLE 09 — WHITE + DEEP BLUE EXECUTIVE BRIEFING
## English Execution Specification | Semantic Expansion + Display Compression | Multi-Page Adaptive Version

# 00 | EXECUTION ORDER

This file is an execution specification for image-generation models.

Apply instructions in this priority order:

1. Preserve source meaning, facts, scope, responsibility, conditions, boundaries and claim strength.
2. Interpret and expand the business semantics internally before composing the page.
3. Define the page mission: determine the one main judgment, relationship or structure the page must communicate.
4. Perform information prioritization and DISPLAY COMPRESSION before any visual composition.
5. Identify the dominant semantic relationship and select the matching composition archetype.
6. Establish one dominant visual focus and one clear reading path.
7. Integrate realistic electric-power / business scenes with the information structure.
8. Apply Style 09 visual DNA consistently.
9. Enforce all readability, restraint and prohibition rules.

Critical rule:

**SEMANTIC EXPANSION DOES NOT AUTHORIZE TEXT EXPANSION.**

Semantic expansion is for internal understanding, relationship discovery and visual design.  
It must not automatically increase the amount of on-page copy.

Do not expose internal analysis, semantic expansion, information prioritization, layout reasoning or design deliberation on the page.

The page must feel like a refined executive-report infographic, not a dashboard, poster, SaaS interface, icon diagram or decorative technology illustration.

# 03 | SEMANTIC EXPANSION BEFORE VISUAL DESIGN

Before composing the page, understand the source as a complete business statement. Do not immediately convert source bullets into shapes or text blocks.

Internally identify:
- core judgment;
- page mission;
- main business object;
- actors / stakeholders;
- inputs and outputs;
- actions and dependencies;
- sequence and time relationships;
- causal and comparative relationships;
- governance, ownership and responsibility boundaries;
- evidence and quantitative signals;
- constraints and risks;
- real-world scenes that can represent the meaning.

Use this expansion only to improve:
- semantic understanding;
- relationship discovery;
- structural selection;
- scene selection;
- visual grouping;
- information prioritization.

Do not invent:
- facts or numbers;
- organizations;
- policies;
- slogans;
- conclusions;
- causal claims;
- stronger claims than the source supports.

Critical separation:

**Semantic completeness must be preserved. Textual completeness is not required unless the source explicitly marks copy as locked / verbatim.**

After semantic expansion:

1. identify the page mission;
2. rank information as core / supporting / secondary;
3. remove semantic duplication;
4. compress the display copy;
5. then design the visual structure.

The final page should show the business logic, not the internal reasoning process.

# 04 | SOURCE-CONTENT MODES AND PRESERVATION

Default behavior: treat supplied content as semantic source material that must be understood, prioritized and rewritten into concise Chinese presentation copy.

All on-page presentation copy must remain in Chinese unless the source explicitly requires another language.

Preserve:
- business objects;
- organization names;
- policy terminology;
- facts, numbers and dates;
- conditions and scope;
- responsibilities and ownership;
- status and boundaries;
- constraints;
- claim strength;
- source-supported relationships.

Preserve meaning, not sentence count.

Do not:
- omit source-critical facts or boundaries;
- distort policy or business terminology;
- replace source meaning with slogans;
- exaggerate claim strength;
- convert nuanced business meaning into misleading keywords.

## 04.1 | DEFAULT SOURCE MODE

Unless text is explicitly marked as locked / verbatim, the model may:
- merge repeated statements;
- shorten explanations;
- convert prose into structural labels;
- represent relationships visually instead of repeating them in text;
- select one efficient expression when the same meaning appears as paragraph + bullets + process chain;
- remove explanatory repetition that does not add new business meaning.

Labels such as "完整上屏内容", "核心内容", "页面内容" or similar headings indicate semantic coverage by default. They do not require every sentence to appear verbatim unless the copy is explicitly marked as locked.

## 04.2 | LOCKED COPY MODE

Only copy explicitly marked with instructions such as:
- LOCKED TEXT;
- VERBATIM;
- MUST APPEAR EXACTLY;
- 原文锁定;
- 必须逐字上屏

must remain unchanged.

Locked copy has priority over display compression.

If locked copy creates unavoidable density, preserve the locked copy and simplify surrounding visual elements. Do not silently rewrite locked text.

## 04.3 | READING-TYPE PPT REQUIREMENT

The page must remain understandable without oral explanation.

Achieve reading completeness through a combination of:
- concise copy;
- visible structure;
- scenes;
- business objects;
- connectors;
- process relationships;
- grouping;
- spatial hierarchy.

Do not equate reading completeness with placing every source sentence on the page.

# 04B | DISPLAY COMPRESSION AND ON-SCREEN INFORMATION BUDGET

Display compression is mandatory after semantic expansion and before visual composition.

The objective is:

**preserve semantic completeness while minimizing textual redundancy.**

## 04B.1 | DISPLAY-COMPRESSION PASS

Before rendering, perform the following internal pass:

1. Identify the one page mission.
2. Keep only information required to understand that mission.
3. Preserve essential facts, boundaries, responsibilities, numbers and claim strength.
4. Merge repeated explanations.
5. Remove narration that is already visible through a process, relationship, grouping or scene.
6. Convert secondary explanations into compact labels where meaning remains clear.
7. Use visual structure to carry relationships that do not require full sentences.
8. Check the final copy against the information budget.

Never expose this compression process on the page.

## 04B.2 | NO DUPLICATE EXPRESSION

Do not express the same business meaning simultaneously as:

**paragraph + bullet list + process chain + repeated labels**

When several forms communicate the same logic, select the most efficient form.

Preferred rule:

- use a short conclusion when the message is conceptual;
- use a process chain when sequence is the message;
- use labels when categories are the message;
- use a relationship diagram when interaction is the message;
- use a scene when the real-world operating context is the message;
- use one short boundary statement when a critical condition must remain explicit.

A single semantic point may receive one primary expression and, only when necessary, one short supporting expression.

## 04B.3 | LOCAL TEXT BUDGET

A typical major information region should contain no more than:

- one heading;
- one short subtitle OR one short explanatory sentence;
- one compact flow / label group when structurally necessary.

Avoid placing heading + subtitle + paragraph + bullets + process chain in the same region.

If a process chain already explains "how", the adjacent prose should explain only the key conclusion, purpose or boundary.

If category labels already identify application areas, add descriptions only where the category name is insufficient.

Prefer:
- headings: approximately 4–12 Chinese characters;
- short judgment / subtitle: approximately 8–24 Chinese characters;
- local explanatory sentence: approximately 15–40 Chinese characters;
- secondary labels: approximately 4–12 Chinese characters.

These are execution targets, not visible instructions.

## 04B.4 | PAGE-LEVEL INFORMATION BUDGET

Use the following target ranges for non-locked Chinese on-page copy:

- conclusion / necessity / value page: approximately 80–160 Chinese characters;
- standard structure / capability page: approximately 150–250 Chinese characters;
- process / mechanism page: approximately 150–280 Chinese characters;
- complex architecture / ecosystem page: approximately 250–400 Chinese characters;
- quantitative page: vary according to the number of source-critical metrics, while keeping explanatory prose minimal.

These ranges are targets rather than absolute limits.

Exceed them only when source-critical facts, numbers, responsibilities, boundaries or locked text cannot otherwise be preserved.

For a complex architecture page, prefer approximately:
- 8–12 major text clusters;
- concise layer labels;
- short relationship labels;
- minimal explanatory prose.

## 04B.5 | OVERFLOW FAIL-SAFE

If the source contains more information than the page can legibly display:

1. preserve the page mission;
2. preserve source-critical facts and boundaries;
3. merge semantic repetition;
4. compress secondary explanations;
5. represent supported relationships visually;
6. remove redundant narration;
7. simplify secondary decorative elements.

Do not solve information overload by:
- shrinking typography into micro-text;
- adding more cards;
- creating dense footnote-like blocks;
- multiplying icons;
- adding extra process labels;
- reducing white space until the page becomes crowded.

**Do not shrink typography to accommodate excess copy.**

If readable display and non-locked source completeness conflict, prioritize readable semantic completeness over sentence-level completeness.

## 04A.2 | DO NOT REDUCE THE SUBJECT TO A SOFTWARE PLATFORM

When visualizing electric-power data infrastructure, do not automatically represent the subject as a single software platform, data middle platform, trading screen, database or AI system.

Treat the infrastructure as an integrated industry-level system that may combine:

- participating organizations;
- data resources;
- trusted circulation mechanisms;
- technical capabilities;
- governance rules;
- application scenarios;
- products and services;
- operating relationships.

A platform may be one structural carrier, but it must not automatically become the whole meaning of the page.

If the source focuses on a platform function, emphasize the platform. If the source focuses on multi-party relationships, circulation, governance, products or scenarios, select a structure that represents those relationships directly.

## 04A.3 | DISTRIBUTED DATA AND CONTROLLED CIRCULATION

Do not automatically visualize data infrastructure as centralized data pooling.

When the source involves trusted circulation, privacy-preserving computation, controlled access, distributed collaboration, trusted connectors or data-space linkage:

- preserve the distinction between data location and service access;
- allow data to remain within the provider's controlled environment when supported by the source;
- visualize trusted connectors, controlled channels, computation or service interfaces when relevant;
- distinguish raw-data movement from result / model / API / indicator / report delivery;
- keep ownership and control boundaries visually legible;
- avoid implying unrestricted raw-data transfer unless explicitly stated.

When the source states or implies that raw data does not leave its controlled domain, the visual logic should emphasize:

**data retained at source → trusted access / controlled computation → authorized output**

Do not draw every data source flowing into one central database.

## 04A.4 | DOMAIN BUSINESS LOGIC FOR APPLICATION SCENARIOS

For electric-power data-infrastructure application scenarios, internally reconstruct the source through the following semantic chain where relevant:

**business demand

→ trusted circulation / controlled processing

## 04A.5 | DATA PRODUCT AND SERVICE DISTINCTION

When source content differentiates resources, products and services, preserve the distinction visually.

Typical interpretation:

- data resource = underlying data / dataset / indicator / knowledge resource;
- data product = processed, packaged or standardized deliverable;
- data service = callable, subscribable, analytical, model-based or operational service;
- business output = report, API result, model result, prediction, assessment, index, controlled analytical result or other source-supported deliverable.

Do not collapse resource, product and service into a single generic "data" object.

When relevant, show the transformation relationship:

**resource → processing / governance / modeling → product / service → controlled delivery**

# 05 | TEXT AND LOCAL INFORMATION UNITS

Place rewritten text directly beside, above, below, partially within or visibly attached to the related business object, scene or structural relationship.

Each local information unit should communicate one clear semantic function.

A local unit may contain:
- one short heading / judgment;
- one concise explanatory sentence OR one compact structural label group;
- optional evidence / metric / status line only when source-critical;
- the related scene, object or relationship.

Prefer:
- conclusion-first wording;
- short sentences;
- compact labels for parallel categories;
- strong local grouping;
- visible relationships instead of repeated narration.

Avoid:
- disconnected labels;
- keyword-only reduction that destroys meaning;
- excessive micro-text;
- repeated title-body-card patterns;
- paragraph + bullet + flow duplication;
- explanatory prose that merely restates visible structure.

Deep-blue headings should be stronger than body text but remain editorial rather than poster-like.

Body copy must remain comfortably readable at normal presentation viewing size.

# 06 | DOMINANT SEMANTIC RELATIONSHIP

Identify the primary relationship before selecting the layout:

- center-to-periphery → radial / hub-and-spoke;
- sequence / circulation → main chain / process;
- explicit time progression → timeline / roadmap;
- cause-to-result → causal expansion;
- alternative-to-alternative → asymmetric comparison;
- whole-to-capabilities → main body + capability clusters;
- judgment-to-proof → conclusion + evidence;
- real-world operation → scene-led composition;
- number-to-argument → key metric + trend / evidence;
- responsibility / governance / boundary → relationship axis + support band.

Never force radial or central-platform composition onto inherently sequential, temporal, comparative, causal, quantitative or scene-based content.

Never create repeated cards merely because the source contains several bullets.

Use only the semantic regions required by the content. Prefer fewer, unequally weighted regions.

Every page must establish one strong semantic and visual anchor.

The dominant focus may be:
- core conclusion;
- platform or key business object;
- major metric;
- business scene;
- critical relationship;
- principal mechanism;
- current / target stage;

Maintain hierarchy:
1. dominant focus;
2. major supporting directions / evidence / stages;
3. secondary actors / objects / details;
4. cross-cutting constraints / governance / implementation support.

Supporting information must remain subordinate but fully readable. Avoid equal visual weight across all regions.

# 09 | STRUCTURAL CARRIERS, CONNECTORS AND DEPTH

Use broad white fields, shallow bases, restrained circles / arcs, flat architectural planes, semi-transparent business carriers and precise editorial alignment.

Use:
- deep-blue primary connectors;
- pale-blue secondary connectors;
- thin precise lines;
- large-radius arcs;
- short arrows;
- restrained dotted relationships;
- subtle circular guides;
- clean directional paths.

Every connector must explain business logic.

Keep depth subtle, matte and architectural.

Avoid glossy 3D, sci-fi platforms, excessive perspective, floating objects, neon effects and game-like rendering.

Align text blocks, scene edges, connectors, headings and structural carriers to an invisible editorial grid.

Use white space to separate semantic layers instead of adding boxes.

Where a region needs definition, prefer a very light outline, thin divider, broad white field or softly faded scene boundary.

# 10 | COMPOSITION ARCHETYPE LIBRARY

Select one primary archetype according to the dominant semantic relationship, not the page title.

## 10.1 RADIAL / HUB-AND-SPOKE
Best for: positioning / architecture / ecosystem / multi-stakeholder relationships.

Use only when a genuine center-to-periphery relationship exists.

Structure:
- center / upper-center = core positioning / platform / principal object;
- middle = 2–4 major directions / capabilities / mechanisms;
- outer = stakeholders / service objects / outcomes / application domains;
- optional bottom band = cross-cutting coordination / implementation / constraints / existing-system linkage.

Use restrained radial logic and pale-blue arcs. Do not use without a genuine center.

## 10.2 CONCLUSION + EVIDENCE
Best for: background / necessity / core judgment / value explanation.

Structure:
- one dominant conclusion;
- 2–4 unequally weighted evidence regions;
- 1–2 realistic scenes supporting evidence;
- optional small quantitative proof / outcome region.

Keep the page spacious. Do not convert evidence into equal cards.

## 10.3 MAIN-CHAIN PROCESS
Best for: business process / data flow / implementation mechanism / service loop.

Structure:
- one visible main chain;
- 3–6 meaningful stages;
- larger text / scenes only at critical stages;
- stronger terminal emphasis on output / business result.

The chain may be horizontal, gently curved, stepped or folded. Embed scenes along the chain. Avoid identical process boxes.

## 10.4 TIMELINE / ROADMAP
Best for: phased construction / roadmap / annual plan / evolution.

Structure:
- one clear time carrier;
- several unequal stages;
- current or target stage emphasized;
- milestones and deliverables close to relevant time points;
- small scenes only where useful.

Avoid generic equal-node timelines.

## 10.5 SCENE-LED
Best for: application scenario / customer case / business solution / co-construction.

Structure:
- one dominant realistic scene;
- actors, inputs, platform actions and outputs annotated directly around / within the scene;
- few connectors;
- one concise conclusion / value region.

The scene itself becomes the page skeleton. Do not surround it with unrelated cards.

## 10.6 CAUSAL ANALYSIS
Best for: problem / cause / constraint / risk propagation.

Structure:
- one dominant problem / constraint / consequence;
- 2–4 upstream causes or conditions;
- clear directional relationships;
- optional impact / response / boundary region.

Use muted amber only for genuine risks, exceptions or constraints.

## 10.7 ASYMMETRIC COMPARISON
Best for: model comparison / option selection / before-after / advantages-disadvantages.

Structure:
- unequal comparison zones;
- one dominant recommendation / preferred direction / key difference when supported;
- shared criteria aligned across the page;
- realistic scenes only at meaningful differences.

Avoid symmetrical side-by-side cards when the conclusion is not balanced.

## 10.8 CAPABILITY SYSTEM
Best for: product system / service system / capability framework / operating system.

Structure:
- one main business objective / platform / service object;
- capability clusters arranged by dependency or business proximity;
- shared infrastructure / support in bottom or background carrier;
- customer-facing outcome shown distinctly.

Avoid icon grids. Use text, business objects, restrained scenes and structural grouping.

## 10.9 QUANTITATIVE / METRIC-LED
Best for: scale / achievement / market / operation data / key indicators.

Structure:
- one dominant metric / trend / quantitative judgment;
- 2–4 supporting numbers / evidence points;
- restrained chart-like carriers if needed;
- realistic scene only as context.

Keep the page editorial, not dashboard-like. Avoid KPI-card collections.

## 10.10 GOVERNANCE / RESPONSIBILITY
Best for: operating mechanism / collaboration / boundaries / division of responsibilities / governance.

Structure:
- one central relationship axis / operating mechanism / responsibility carrier;
- parties positioned by actual relationship, not symmetry;
- rights, responsibilities, control boundaries and ownership attached directly to the relevant relationship;
- optional bottom support band for governance rules / shared capabilities / implementation modes.

Use precise connectors and restrained boundary lines. Avoid organization-chart aesthetics unless genuinely hierarchical.

---

## 10.11 TRUSTED CIRCULATION / MULTI-PARTY COLLABORATION
Best for: trusted data circulation / multi-party collaboration / data-space linkage / privacy-preserving computation / joint modeling / cross-organization data services.

Use when the core meaning depends on multiple actors retaining distinct resource or control domains while collaborating through trusted mechanisms.

Preferred structure:
- multiple real business actors remain visually distinct;
- each actor may retain its own data / system / resource domain;
- trusted relationships connect actors through a shared service, collaboration or infrastructure mechanism;
- authorization, controlled processing, computation or service invocation occurs in the relationship layer;
- outputs such as indicators, models, reports, APIs or services are visually separated from raw-data resources;
- ownership, authorization and control boundaries remain legible;
- business outcome receives clear terminal emphasis.

Preferred semantic logic:

**distributed resources  
→ trusted connection / authorization / controlled collaboration  
→ processing / computation / service mechanism  
→ controlled output  
→ business value**

Do not:
- automatically move all source data into one central repository;
- imply unrestricted raw-data exchange;
- draw a generic blockchain network;
- replace business actors with abstract nodes;
- turn trusted circulation into decorative flowing lines.

When a shared platform exists, represent it as a collaboration / service carrier unless the source explicitly defines centralized data storage as its main role.

---

# 11 | BOTTOM SUPPORT BAND

Use only when genuine cross-cutting information supports multiple major regions, such as:
- existing-system linkage;
- implementation modes;
- governance rules;
- common technical support;
- ownership boundaries;
- security constraints;
- supporting conditions;
- shared infrastructure.

The band must function as a structural carrier, not another card wall.

It may use thin dividers, sparse labels, very small monochrome symbols, one or two concise text groups and one subtle line back to the main body.

Do not create it without genuine cross-cutting semantics.

---

# 12 | ICON AND DEVICE CONTROL

Use icons only when they materially improve recognition.

Typical page: no more than 1–2 icons.

Icons must be simple, deep blue, small and integrated into an existing text / scene / structural region.

Do not give icons independent cards, circular badges, decorative containers or dedicated display zones.

Icons must not determine composition, create extra modules, form decorative rows / process sequences, or replace scenes, business objects or source-backed text.

Screens and devices may appear only as supporting evidence inside a broader working scene.

Avoid repeated devices, isolated UI screenshots, dashboards, SaaS interfaces and product-display layouts.

# 14 | FINAL EXECUTION CHECK

Before rendering, verify:

## Meaning
- source meaning is preserved;
- facts, numbers, scope, responsibilities and boundaries are unchanged;
- no unsupported conclusion is added;
- Chinese wording remains semantically complete.

## Semantic Expansion
- semantic expansion improved understanding rather than increasing on-page copy;
- internal reasoning is not exposed;
- inferred relationships are source-supported.

## Display Compression
- the page mission is clear;
- core / supporting / secondary information has been prioritized;
- repeated meanings have been merged;
- no paragraph + bullet + process duplication remains;
- the page-level copy is within or reasonably close to the target information budget;
- local text blocks are concise.

## Structure
- one dominant semantic relationship is identified;
- the selected archetype matches it;
- the page is not forced into a generic template;
- semantic regions are few and unequally weighted.

## Focus
- one dominant visual focus exists;
- supporting content is subordinate;
- the reading path is immediately clear.

## Scene
- every scene supports business meaning;
- scenes are integrated rather than pasted;
- no decorative photography appears.

## Text
- all source-critical copy is readable;
- locked copy, if any, is preserved exactly;
- text is attached to the relevant object / scene / relationship;
- the page is understandable without oral explanation;
- typography has not been reduced to micro-text to fit excess content.

## Precision
- connectors are thin and purposeful;
- alignment is editorial and precise;
- white space is generous;
- visual depth is restrained.

## Restraint
- no card wall;
- no icon grid;
- no dashboard;
- no glossy 3D;
- no excessive decoration;
- no text-density rescue through smaller type or more boxes.
