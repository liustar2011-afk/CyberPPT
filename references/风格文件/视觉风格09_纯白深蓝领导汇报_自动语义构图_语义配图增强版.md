# 视觉风格09：纯白 + 深蓝领导汇报｜自动语义构图版

## 01｜视觉身份

Palette: white #FFFFFF, deep blue #12355B, muted amber #D9772B, title #12355B, body #303030, secondary #6F7275, divider #C9CDD1.

Create a high-end senior leadership briefing page in a structured editorial business-infographic style: authoritative, calm, polished, precise, restrained and content-led.

The page should feel like a refined executive-report infographic, not a dashboard, poster, product interface, generic architecture diagram or icon-based template.

Use deep blue for primary relationships, major structural carriers, key judgments and conclusions. Reserve muted amber only for genuine risks, exceptions, constraints, pending states and occasional decision-critical emphasis. Never use amber as a category color or decorative accent.

---

# 02｜核心原则：统一视觉语言，不统一页面骨架

Maintain a consistent visual language across the full presentation, but allow the page skeleton to change according to the meaning of the content.

Every page should preserve the following visual DNA:

- pure-white background;
- restrained deep-blue hierarchy;
- one visually dominant focus;
- asymmetric and unequally weighted composition;
- one clear reading path;
- strong semantic hierarchy;
- thin, precise connectors and dividers;
- generous white space;
- refined editorial alignment;
- few major visual structures;
- minimal decorative graphics;
- realistic scenes only when they add semantic value;
- icons strictly subordinate to structure, text and scenes.

Do not force visual consistency by repeating the same layout.

Consistency must come from hierarchy, spacing, color, linework, scene treatment, typography and editorial discipline.

---

# 03｜自动语义解析与构图路由｜高优先级

Before rendering, silently infer the visual structure directly from the meaning of the supplied on-screen content.

Do not require:
- a separate page-type label;
- a manually written visual-semantic note;
- a predefined layout instruction;
- a keyword trigger;
- a fixed composition template.

Construct an internal semantic graph of the page.

Identify:

- the principal object, judgment, scene, metric or relationship;
- major subordinate objects;
- supporting details;
- actors, resources, inputs, actions, outputs, evidence, constraints and outcomes when present;
- the relationships among these elements;
- which relationship organizes the page;
- which relationships only modify, constrain, support or explain the primary structure.

Infer these relationships from the full meaning of the source copy rather than from individual words, headings, sentence order, paragraph length or bullet count.

Conceptually distinguish relationship types such as:

- containment and hierarchy;
- dependency and support;
- sequence and flow;
- temporal progression;
- convergence and divergence;
- mapping and correspondence;
- cross-cutting or shared governance;
- comparison and contrast;
- cause and consequence;
- center-to-periphery service relationships;
- composition and capability aggregation;
- evidence supporting a conclusion;
- actor-resource-action-output relationships;
- ownership, control, responsibility and boundary relationships.

These are semantic relationships, not keyword-triggered templates.

---

# 04｜主关系与次关系裁决

When several semantic relationships coexist, determine which single relationship should organize the page.

Select exactly one primary structural logic.

Secondary relationships may appear only as:
- overlays;
- connectors;
- boundaries;
- annotations;
- background carriers;
- shared support bands;
- subtle cross-cutting structures.

Do not convert every relationship type into an independent visual region.

Visual weight must follow semantic dependency:

- the structure that organizes the other information receives the greatest weight;
- relationships that act across that structure remain visually lighter;
- descriptive details remain embedded within their semantic parent;
- supporting evidence remains subordinate to the main structure;
- contextual scenes never compete with the primary semantic relationship.

If several systems describe the same subject from different dimensions, determine whether one is the primary structure and the others are orthogonal views or cross-cutting relationships.

When this is the case:

- preserve one system as the dominant visual skeleton;
- integrate the other dimensions across, through, beside or within that skeleton;
- show correspondence where meaningful;
- avoid placing each dimension into a disconnected parallel panel unless the content genuinely describes independent systems.

Multiple views of the same underlying subject should feel integrated rather than adjacent.

---

# 05｜基于语义拓扑选择构图

Choose the composition from the topology of the inferred semantic graph.

The following are visual topology options, not page-title rules and not keyword triggers.

### A. Layered structure

Use when the semantic graph is dominated by hierarchy, containment, dependency or progressive support.

Create one clear vertical, horizontal or stepped structural hierarchy.

Avoid equal stacked cards. Use scale, spacing and emphasis to distinguish foundational, transitional and outward-facing parts.

### B. Main-chain structure

Use when the semantic graph is dominated by ordered transformation, directional flow or an end-to-end business relationship.

Create one visible main chain.

Embed key objects, scenes and explanations along the chain instead of drawing a row of identical process boxes.

### C. Radial structure

Use when one genuine central object connects to several relatively independent surrounding objects.

Create one strong central anchor with restrained outward relationships.

Do not use radial composition merely because several parallel items exist.

### D. Conclusion-evidence structure

Use when several facts, conditions or business realities primarily support one judgment.

Give the conclusion dominant visual weight and arrange evidence asymmetrically around or beneath it.

Avoid converting each evidence point into an equal card.

### E. Scene-led structure

Use when actors, resources, actions and outcomes form one coherent real-world operating situation.

Let the scene become the structural carrier and attach information directly to meaningful business objects or relationships.

### F. Comparative structure

Use when several objects, modes or states are evaluated through shared dimensions.

Use unequal visual weight when the source supports a preferred direction, key difference or stronger conclusion.

Avoid mechanical symmetrical comparison.

### G. Causal structure

Use when upstream conditions explain a downstream state, problem, effect or consequence.

Make the causal direction visually legible with few, purposeful relationships.

### H. Temporal structure

Use when the dominant organization is change over time, phases, milestones or evolution.

Use a clear time carrier, but vary scale and spacing according to importance.

Avoid generic equal-node timelines.

### I. Capability-system structure

Use when multiple functional clusters combine to support one business objective, service object or operating capability.

Organize capabilities by dependency, proximity or business role rather than by icon grids.

### J. Governance-and-boundary structure

Use when responsibility, ownership, control, coordination, rights, obligations or operating boundaries dominate the page.

Use relationship axes, boundary fields, shared carriers and precise connectors.

Avoid defaulting to an organization chart unless the semantics are genuinely hierarchical.

---

# 06｜语义压缩：保留信息，不膨胀视觉对象

Preserve all required on-screen meaning, but do not assign a separate visual object to every noun, function, capability or list item.

Distinguish between:

1. structural information that determines geometry;
2. relationship information that determines connectors or spatial interaction;
3. descriptive information that should remain text;
4. evidence or scene information that may receive visual support.

Visualize structure and relationships first.

Keep subordinate capability names, attributes and descriptive lists as compact text within or beside their semantic parent unless they materially change the page structure.

A dense page may contain many textual concepts while still using only a small number of major visual structures.

Do not transform long lists into:
- icon walls;
- repeated cards;
- badge arrays;
- tile grids;
- menu-like functional panels.

The number of visual objects should reflect the number of major semantic structures, not the number of textual concepts.

---


# 08｜语义配图规则｜高优先级

When using scenes, photographic fragments or realistic visual objects, prioritize local semantic relevance over generic industry recognition.

An image should first explain the function, role, action, object, evidence or relationship of the content region it supports, and only secondarily signal the broader industry category.

Every scene, object image or photographic fragment must be semantically bound to its parent content region.

A supporting image should represent at least one of the following:
- the specific business role of the region;
- the managed or operated object;
- the operational action taking place;
- the physical or professional environment in which the activity occurs;
- the evidence, result or condition being discussed;
- the relationship between actors, resources, systems or outputs.

Do not use a generic industry image merely because it belongs to the same sector.

A scene is semantically weak when it could be replaced by another random image from the same industry without materially changing the meaning of the page.

Prefer visual evidence that is specific to the local semantic unit.

## Local semantic binding

Treat every image as part of a local text-and-visual semantic unit.

The image and nearby text should answer the same business question.

The image must be physically close to the content it supports and should visually reinforce that region rather than serve as a general page background.

Do not separate a region's text and its supporting scene so far apart that the semantic connection becomes ambiguous.

Do not assign one image to several unrelated content regions unless the source meaning genuinely describes one shared context.

## Visual specificity hierarchy

When visual support is useful, prefer the most semantically specific form available:

1. operation-specific visual evidence;
2. object-specific visual evidence;
3. environment-specific visual evidence;
4. generic industry-context imagery.

Use generic industry-context imagery only when the broader physical setting itself is relevant.

Prefer object-specific, operation-specific or evidence-specific visuals over generic landscape-style industry photography.

Infrastructure landscapes are appropriate only when the meaning is genuinely about physical infrastructure, geographic deployment, asset scale, operating context or industry environment.

For abstract management, governance, platform, process, capability or service concepts, prefer:
- a relevant working environment;
- a managed business object;
- a concrete operational artifact;
- a restrained structural visual;
- or no image at all.

Do not force a landscape photograph to represent an abstract management concept.

## Scene necessity test

Before adding any scene, silently ask:

- Does this image explain something that the text alone does not show visually?
- Does it reinforce the exact local business meaning?
- Would replacing it with another random industry photograph weaken the meaning?
- Does it help distinguish this region from neighboring regions?

If the answer is no, omit the image.

If no realistic scene can add precise semantic value to a region, reduce or omit the scene rather than using a generic category image.

White space, structure and text are preferable to semantically weak decoration.

## Scene density control

Scene quantity must follow semantic need, not style compliance.

Do not assign one generic industry image to every structural block.

On abstract architecture, system-relationship, governance, capability-system or mechanism pages:
- use only a few semantically meaningful scenes or photographic fragments;
- scenes may be omitted entirely;
- one contextual scene may support several closely related elements only when the shared context is genuine;
- avoid repeated power-industry photographs used merely to fill visual space.

On scenario, customer, operational or application pages:
- scenes may become the main structural carrier;
- multiple scene fragments may be used when they represent different concrete business roles or actions;
- each fragment must have a clear local semantic responsibility.

## Scene-object integration

When possible, attach text to specific meaningful objects or subregions inside a scene rather than placing text beside a generic photograph.

Prefer:
- annotation tied to a real business object;
- partial scene crops focused on the relevant operation;
- embedded scene fragments aligned with the semantic region;
- object-level evidence;
- environment fragments that reveal the operating context.

Avoid:
- full-width decorative landscapes without semantic responsibility;
- repetitive stock-like industry scenes;
- visually impressive but semantically interchangeable photography;
- scenic images that compete with the page's primary structural logic.

## Semantic diversity across scenes

When several scenes appear on one page, they should represent meaningfully different business roles, operational contexts or evidence.

Do not use multiple visually different images that all convey only the same generic message such as “energy”, “technology”, “digitalization” or “power industry”.

Visual diversity must reflect semantic diversity.

## Relationship between scene and abstraction

Use realistic scenes to ground abstract concepts only when the scene can make the abstraction more concrete.

Do not attempt to literalize every abstract concept with a photograph.

When the page's main value lies in architecture, governance, coordination, mapping, ownership, control or capability relationships, prioritize structural clarity over scene richness.

When the page's main value lies in a real operating situation, customer interaction, production process or physical system, allow scenes to carry more visual weight.

# 09｜文字与信息组织


Rewrite the supplied source copy into concise, conclusion-first Chinese presentation text when compression is needed for visual readability.

Preserve:
- facts;
- business objects;
- numbers;
- conditions;
- scope;
- responsibility;
- status;
- claim strength;
- logical relationships.

Do not invent unsupported facts, slogans, conclusions, policy language or business claims.

Place text directly beside, above, below or partially within the related business object, structural carrier or scene so that text and visual material form one local content unit.

Prefer:
- conclusion-first wording;
- compact paragraphs;
- short explanatory phrases;
- bullets only when parallel items genuinely need separation;
- strong local grouping.

Do not reduce complete business meaning into isolated keywords merely to simplify the page.

Do not compress required body copy merely to create a visual center.

The page should remain understandable without oral explanation.

---

# 10｜实景使用：按语义价值调用，不强制每页出现

Style 09 is scene-capable, not scene-mandatory.

Use realistic scenes only when a physical environment, actor, operation, infrastructure object or real-world business context materially improves understanding.

Use realistic, clean and context-rich scenes such as:
- grid facilities;
- substations;
- dispatch centers;
- control rooms;
- transmission systems;
- renewable-energy bases;
- storage equipment;
- power plants;
- industrial facilities;
- professional analysis environments;
- operational resources;
- business collaboration environments.

For abstract, conceptual, structural, governance-oriented or relationship-driven pages:

- scenes may be omitted entirely;
- or use only one low-contrast contextual scene when it adds genuine meaning;
- never add several unrelated industry photographs merely to signal the industry category.

For scenario, customer, application or operational pages:

- scenes may become a major structural carrier;
- actors, resources, actions and outputs may be attached directly to real business objects in the scene.

Each scene must carry semantic evidence, context or business meaning.

Never use photography simply to fill space.

Integrate scenes through:
- soft edge fading;
- cropped photographic windows;
- partial background embedding;
- restrained white-to-image transitions;
- shallow overlap with adjacent text or structure.

Avoid placing every scene inside a rigid card.

People should be absent or minimal. If present, keep them small, non-portrait and subordinate to the environment.

# 11｜视觉焦点与空间层级

Every page must establish one visually dominant focus that makes the core judgment, object or relationship immediately recognizable.

The focus may be:
- a conclusion;
- a key metric;
- a business object;
- a scene;
- a critical relationship;
- a platform;
- a stage;
- a mechanism;
- a risk state.

Express dominance through:
- scale;
- position;
- contrast;
- deep blue;
- restrained scene scale;
- structural carrier;
- semantic centrality;
- limited muted amber only when justified.

Supporting information must remain subordinate while fully readable.

Maintain visible hierarchy:

1. dominant semantic focus;
2. major supporting structures or relationships;
3. secondary objects and details;
4. cross-cutting support, constraints or contextual information.

Do not let a decorative object, photograph, icon group or dark-filled container become the visual focus unless it is also the semantic focus.

---

# 12｜连接关系与精细度

Use deep-blue primary connectors and pale-blue secondary connectors.

Favor:
- thin precise lines;
- large-radius arcs;
- restrained directional paths;
- short arrows;
- subtle dotted relationships;
- clean boundary lines;
- light structural guides.

Connectors must explain actual semantic relationships.

Do not use lines to decorate empty space.

Words describing relationships in the source must influence page geometry.

If the content expresses:
- dependency, show dependency;
- mapping, show correspondence;
- convergence, show convergence;
- cross-cutting action, show cross-cutting action;
- hierarchy, show hierarchy;
- flow, show direction;
- boundary, show boundary;
- shared support, show shared support.

Never replace an important semantic relationship with mere proximity.

Use thin dividers, precise alignment, controlled spacing and consistent geometry.

Text blocks, scene edges, connectors and structural carriers should align to an invisible editorial grid.

Avoid thick borders and heavy framing.

Where a region needs definition, prefer:
- a very light blue-gray outline;
- a thin divider;
- a broad white field;
- a softly faded scene boundary.

---

# 13｜结构载体与视觉深度

Use broad flat fields and pure-white content regions.

When a structural carrier is needed, it may use:
- a shallow platform;
- a restrained ring;
- a flat architectural plane;
- a linear rail;
- a thin band;
- a semi-transparent structural field.

Keep all depth subtle and matte.

Avoid:
- glossy 3D;
- sci-fi platforms;
- excessive perspective;
- floating objects;
- neon technology effects;
- glassmorphism;
- game-like visual language.

Depth should strengthen hierarchy, not become a visual subject.

---

# 14｜图标与设备约束

Use icons only when they materially improve recognition.

Keep icons:
- simple;
- small;
- monochrome or deep blue;
- embedded within an existing semantic region.

Icons must never determine the composition.

Do not create:
- icon grids;
- icon walls;
- repeated circular badges;
- independent icon cards;
- decorative icon rows;
- menu-like feature panels.

Screens and devices may appear only as supporting evidence inside a broader working scene.

Avoid:
- isolated UI screenshots;
- dashboards;
- SaaS interfaces;
- product-display layouts;
- repeated device mockups.

Avoid composition patterns that contradict the source semantics.

# 16｜自动构图自检｜最高优先级

Before rendering, silently verify:

1. What is the single most important semantic relationship or object on this page?
2. Does the visual skeleton represent that relationship directly?
3. Is there exactly one dominant structural logic?
4. Are secondary relationships integrated according to their actual role?
5. Does visual weight correspond to semantic importance?
6. Have descriptive lists remained subordinate rather than becoming cards, icons or modules?
7. Are independent visual regions genuinely independent in meaning?
8. Are orthogonal views integrated rather than merely placed side by side?
9. Are scenes contributing precise local semantic value rather than only generic industry recognition?
10. Is every image clearly bound to the content region it supports?
11. Could any scene be replaced by a random image from the same industry without changing the meaning? If yes, remove or replace it.
12. Is the scene density justified by semantic need?
13. Can the major relationship of the page be understood before reading every small line of text?
14. Has the page become visually dense because too many nouns were converted into graphics?
15. Would removing any decorative object make the semantic structure clearer?

If any answer indicates semantic-visual mismatch, simplify and restructure before rendering.

---

# 17｜最终执行原则

The page skeleton must follow the dominant semantic relationship of the content.

Do not force all pages into one composition.

Do not rely on keyword matching to select layouts.

Do not use bullet count, paragraph count or sentence order as a proxy for visual importance.

Do not add unrelated decorative objects.

Do not compress required body copy merely to create a focus.

Unify the visual language, not the page skeleton.

Across a complete presentation, Style 09 should remain recognizable through:

- pure white + deep blue executive-report aesthetics;
- one dominant focus per page;
- semantic-first composition;
- asymmetric editorial hierarchy;
- refined pale-blue structural relationships;
- realistic scenes only when meaningful;
- restrained matte depth;
- precise alignment;
- generous white space;
- high readability;
- minimal decorative graphics.

Final result: a calm, highly readable, presentation-ready executive-report visual system in which Image2 autonomously infers the most appropriate page structure from the supplied Chinese on-screen content, while preserving a consistent Style 09 visual identity across diverse page types.
